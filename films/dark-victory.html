Dark Victory

1939 ★ 99 min
Starring: Ronald Reagan, Humphrey Bogart, Bette Davis
Director: Edmund Goulding
Writer: Casey Robinson
Producer: Hal B. Wallis, David Lewis

Dark Victory is a 1939 American melodrama film directed by Edmund Goulding, starring Bette Davis, and featuring George Brent, Humphrey Bogart, Geraldine Fitzgerald, Ronald Reagan, Henry Travers, and Cora Witherspoon. The screenplay by Casey Robinson was based on the 1934 play of the same title by George Brewer and Bertram Bloch, starring Tallulah Bankhead.

Trivia

  • Nominated for: Academy Award for Best Picture; Academy Award for Best Actress; Academy Award for Best Original Score.
  • Based on Dark Victory (1934 Broadway play written by George Brewer Jr. and Bertram Bloch).
  • Tallulah Bankhead originated the role of Judith Traherne in the Broadway production, which ran for 51 performances at the Plymouth Theatre, before being cut short when Bankhead fell ill with a bacterial infection.
  • Davis openly admitted in later years that she had emulated Bankhead in the role.
  • In 1935, David O. Selznick wanted to cast Greta Garbo and Fredric March in the leads, but Garbo chose to play the lead in Anna Karenina instead.
