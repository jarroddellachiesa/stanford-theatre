Tabu: A Story of the South Seas

1931 ★ 81 min
Studio: Fox Film Corporation
Starring: Anne Chevalier
Director: F. W. Murnau
Writer: Robert J. Flaherty, Edgar G. Ulmer, F. W. Murnau
Producer: Robert J. Flaherty

Tabu: A Story of the South Seas (pronounced [ˈtapu]) is a 1931 American synchronized sound film directed by F. W. Murnau. While the film has no audible dialog, it was released with a synchronized musical score with sound effects using the Western Electric Sound System sound-on-film process.

Trivia

  • Won the Academy Award for Best Cinematography.
  • Selected for the National Board of Review: Top Ten Films.
  • Production began in January 1930, with Flaherty directing the opening scene of the film.
  • Flaherty began having technical problems, as his camera was causing the film to rip.
  • Murnau, Flaherty, and Crosby were the only professional filmmakers working on Tabu: A Story of the South Seas; the rest of the crew was made up of local natives.

Filming Locations

  • Tahiti
