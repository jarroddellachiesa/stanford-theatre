Lassie Come Home

1943 ★ 89 min
Starring: Roddy McDowall, Elsa Lanchester, Donald Crisp
Director: Fred M. Wilcox
Writer: Hugo Butler
Producer: Samuel Marx

Lassie Come Home is a 1943 American, Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer Technicolor drama film starring Roddy McDowall and canine actor Pal, in a story about the profound bond between Yorkshire boy Joe Carraclough and his rough collie, Lassie. The film was directed by Fred M. Wilcox from a screenplay by Hugo Butler based upon the 1940 novel Lassie Come-Home by Eric Knight.

Trivia

  • Selected for the National Board of Review: Top Ten Films.
  • Also nominated for: Academy Award for Best Cinematography, Color.
  • Based on Lassie Come-Home (1940 novel by Eric Knight).
  • Additional photography occurred in Big Bear Lake and Cape Mendocino.
  • The trainer, Rudd Weatherwax, then substituted the male collie, Pal, in the role of "Lassie". Pal had been hired to perform the rapids stunt and, being male, looked more impressive in the part.
  • Still other accounts, such as a 1943 New York Times article written while the film was in production, say that Pal was cast by director Fred Wilcox after first being rejected, because no other dog performed satisfactorily with the "near human attributes" he sought for the canine title role.

Filming Locations

  • California
  • Malibu
  • The film was shot in Washington state and Monterey, California, while the rapids scene was shot on the San Joaquin River.
