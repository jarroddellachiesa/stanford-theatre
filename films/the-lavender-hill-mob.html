The Lavender Hill Mob

1951 ★ 78 min
Studio: The Rank Organisation
Starring: Alec Guinness, Stanley Holloway, Audrey Hepburn
Director: Charles Crichton
Writer: T. E. B. Clarke
Producer: Michael Balcon

The Lavender Hill Mob is a 1951 British comedy film from Ealing Studios, written by T. E. B. Clarke, directed by Charles Crichton, starring Alec Guinness and Stanley Holloway and featuring Sid James and Alfie Bass. The title refers to Lavender Hill, a street in Battersea, a district in London SW11, near to Clapham Junction railway station. It was one of the first films to feature Audrey Hepburn, in a minor role.

Trivia

  • Won the Academy Award for Best Writing, Original Screenplay.
  • Won the BAFTA Award for Best British Film.
  • Also nominated for: Academy Award for Best Actor; BAFTA Award for Best Film; Directors Guild of America Award for Outstanding Directing – Feature Film.
  • Charles Crichton says the origin of the film came with the success of The Blue Lamp.
  • Michael Balcon, head of Ealing, got screenwriter Clarke to come up with ideas for a follow-up.
  • Clarke is said to have come up with the idea of a clerk robbing his own bank while doing research for the film Pool of London (1951), a crime thriller surrounding a jewel theft.
