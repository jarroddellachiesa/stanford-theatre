Picnic

1955 ★ 115 min

1955 film by Joshua Logan

Picnic is a 1955 American romantic comedy-drama film directed by Joshua Logan, adapted by Daniel Taradash from William Inge's 1953 Pulitzer Prize-winning play. It stars William Holden, Kim Novak, and Rosalind Russell, with Betty Field, Susan Strasberg, and Cliff Robertson in supporting roles. The film dramatizes 24 hours in the life of a small Kansas town in the mid-20th century during the Labor Day holiday.

Trivia

  • Won the Academy Award for Best Film Editing.
  • Won the Academy Award for Best Art Direction, Color.
  • Selected for the National Board of Review: Top Ten Films.
  • Also nominated for: Academy Award for Best Picture; Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor; Academy Award for Best Director; Academy Award for Best Original Dramatic or Comedy Score.
  • Columbia Pictures acquired the screen rights to William Inge's play Picnic for $350,000 in September 1953.
  • Daniel Taradash once indicated that Columbia studio head Harry Cohn had offered him the opportunity to direct Picnic in exchange for writing the script.
  • Logan had previously directed I Met My Love Again (1938) and Picnic would be the second feature film he directed in 17 years.
