Sunset Boulevard

1950 ★ 110 min
Studio: Paramount Pictures

Director: Billy Wilder

Producer: Charles Brackett

Sunset Boulevard is a 1950 American black comedy film noir directed by Billy Wilder and co-written by Wilder, Charles Brackett, and D. M. Marshman Jr. It is named after a major street that runs through Hollywood and western Los Angeles.

Trivia

  • Won the Academy Award for Best Writing, Original Screenplay.
  • Won the Academy Award for Best Art Direction, Black and White.
  • Won the Academy Award for Best Original Dramatic or Comedy Score.
  • Selected for the National Board of Review: Top Ten Films.
  • Won the National Board of Review Award for Best Film.
  • Also nominated for: Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress; Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor; Academy Award for Best Actor; Academy Award for Best Actress; and 4 more.
  • Sunset Boulevard in Los Angeles has been associated with Hollywood film production since 1911, when the town's first film studio, Nestor, opened there.
  • Film workers lived modestly in the growing neighborhood, but during the 1920s, profits and salaries rose to unprecedented levels.
  • With the advent of the star system, luxurious homes noted for their often incongruous grandeur were built in the area.
