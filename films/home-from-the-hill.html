Home from the Hill

1960 ★ 149 min
Starring: Robert Mitchum, Eleanor Parker, George Peppard
Director: Vincente Minnelli
Writer: Irving Ravetch, William Humphrey, Harriet Frank Jr.
Producer: Edmund Grainger

Home from the Hill is a 1960 American melodrama Western film starring Robert Mitchum, Eleanor Parker, George Peppard, George Hamilton, Everett Sloane and Luana Patten. Directed by Vincente Minnelli and filmed in Metrocolor and CinemaScope, it was produced by Edward Grainger, and distributed by MGM. The screenplay was adapted from the 1958 novel Home from the Hill by William Humphrey.

Trivia

  • Won the National Board of Review Award for Best Actor.
  • Selected for the National Board of Review: Top Ten Films.
  • Also nominated for: BAFTA Award for Most Promising Newcomer to Leading Film Roles.
  • The writers also tried to capture the cadence of Southern speech and had written another family drama located in the South, The Long, Hot Summer.
  • Minnelli would later call the screenplay "one of the few film scripts in which I didn't change a word." This film was originally intended for Clark Gable and Bette Davis, but the roles then went to Robert Mitchum and Eleanor Parker.
  • The homes used in the film, particularly for the interior shots, are in Oxford, as is the downtown area.

Filming Locations

  • Despite being set in Clarksville, Texas, few scenes were filmed there, though the opening scene shows a hearse driving around Clarksville's downtown square as old men are seen sitting, and whittling pieces of wood, at the base of a monument in the center of the square.
  • Filming took place in Oxford, Mississippi near the University of Mississippi campus, and Paris, Texas and its surrounding area.
  • Other scenes were filmed south of Cuthand, Texas in Red River County.
