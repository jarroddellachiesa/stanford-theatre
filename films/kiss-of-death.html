Kiss of Death

1947 ★ 98 min
Starring: Victor Mature, Brian Donlevy, Coleen Gray
Director: Henry Hathaway
Writer: Ben Hecht, Charles Lederer, Philip Dunne
Producer: Fred Kohlmar, Darryl F. Zanuck

Kiss of Death is a 1947 American film noir directed by Henry Hathaway, written by Ben Hecht and Charles Lederer from a story by Eleazar Lipsky, and starring Victor Mature, Brian Donlevy, Coleen Gray, and Richard Widmark in his film debut. Set in New York City, the film follows a thief involved in a jewelry heist who begins to give up his accomplices after his wife commits suicide while he is in prison.

Trivia

  • Won the Locarno International Film Festival Awards.
  • Also nominated for: Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor; Academy Award for Best Story.
  • Kiss of Death is based on a story by a former district attorney, Lawrence Blaine.
  • Hathaway said the idea to push the lady in the wheelchair down the stairs came from Ben Hecht.
  • According to Widmark, Hathaway disliked his high hairline because he thought it made him look too intellectual, so he ordered Widmark fitted for a hairpiece.

Filming Locations

  • New York City
  • New Jersey
  • Kiss of Death was shot between March and May 1947, with additional scenes being filmed in June.
  • The exterior scenes of the family home were shot in Astoria, Queens, at 14th Place and Astoria Park, and the Triboro Bridge and the Hell Gate Bridge can be seen in the background over Astoria Park.
